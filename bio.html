Bio

I’m a data scientist and machine learning engineer at Faraday. Before moving into industry I was a research mathematician: I earned a Ph.D. at Dartmouth working with John Voight on computational number theory, and spent time as a postdoc at ICERM and as a researcher at ERDC-CRREL.

Current work

At Faraday I partner with clients to prototype and build on the Faraday consumer-prediction platform — ranging from general guidance to custom technical solutions — and contribute to feature development, research, and implementation across our modeling infrastructure and API/UI.

Research background

My mathematical research centered on computational number theory and arithmetic geometry:

  • Belyi maps & dessins d’enfants — Galois Belyi maps with 2-group monodromy, and numerical/database methods for computing them.
  • Hilbert modular forms — data structures for Fourier expansions supporting canonical-ring computations.
  • Mathematical databases — contributions to the LMFDB, including the database of Belyi maps.

Since then I’ve worked on computer vision (landmine detection at CRREL) and consumer-prediction modeling at Faraday.
